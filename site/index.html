COLLABORATIVE ASTROPHOTOGRAPHY

Many backyards.
One deep image.

Shoot one target with dozens of other astrophotographers: a 600-hour H-alpha mosaic of the Veil, or the faint outer halo of M31. At dusk your rig asks what to shoot and gets a panel, filter and exposure that suit its field and filters. Upload your calibrated subs, and they go into a stack no single backyard could build.

HOW YOU TAKE PART

Pair once.
Shoot when it's clear.

You join a project on its website and pair your rig once. On each clear night your capture software asks the server what to shoot, and the server picks the panel with the fewest hours that your rig can frame. You upload your subs when they are ready.

01 / JOIN

Pair your rig

Join a project on its website. Type the pairing code into your capture software, then enter your camera, scope and filters. You do this once for every project you join.

POST /pair ↗ 02 / SHOOT

Shoot the panel you get

At dusk your software asks what to shoot. It gets a target, panel, filter, sub length and frame count, and runs them like any other sequence. It tells the server what you shot, even before you upload.

POST /me/checkins ↗ 03 / UPLOAD

Upload your subs

Calibrate as usual and upload the subs, or your own master if the project wants one. You can share a Google Drive folder instead. The server checks each sub and adds the good ones to the stack.

POST /projects/{id}/submissions ↗

ASSIGNMENTS

Your panel
of the mosaic.

A 2000 mm SCT gets one panel of M31; a 135 mm lens takes the whole galaxy in one frame. A dual-band filter on a color camera can feed the H-alpha and OIII goals at once. As panels fill, the server moves rigs on to whatever is still thin.

  • Panels sized to your field of view
  • Filters matched by passband, mono or OSC
  • Your software stays in charge of the mount

GROUND RULES

Fair to every rig

Read the protocol →
◎

No one owns a target

Two rigs can shoot the same panel on the same night. Good data from both goes in the stack.

⌁

Every good sub counts

Subs are checked for focus, framing and calibration. Each accepted sub counts once toward your hours, whether you send it alone or inside your own master.

↗

Your rig, your call

The server says where the stack needs data. Your capture software decides when to slew, and your safety limits always win.
